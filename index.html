Key ideas

Complex looks, fewer steps.

Editors like CapCut and After Effects show how much expressive editing can do. ModCut takes inspiration from that kind of tooling and focuses on one question: how quickly can you get to a finished, polished look?

01

Effects made approachable

Effects that usually take careful, manual work are designed to be quicker to set up, so more of the time goes into the edit itself.

02

Transitions inspired by real edits

ModCut's transitions were modelled on examples from finished edits. The goal: let users create that style of transition quickly, instead of rebuilding it by hand every time.

03

Describe the style you want

A downloadable, pre-existing AI model helps interpret short requests, such as “presentation”, and turn them into results in the intended visual style.

AI workflow

Built through iteration.

ModCut was developed with Claude Code and another AI coding agent. My job was to set the direction, explain what “right” looks like, and keep refining until the results matched the vision.

  1. 1

    Vision

    Define what a feature should feel like for the person editing.

  2. 2

    Prompts & examples

    Write prompts and show reference examples so the AI tools know the target.

  3. 3

    Review & test

    Run the result, compare it with the intent, and note what is off.

  4. 4

    Feedback

    Give specific feedback, combine good AI suggestions with my own ideas, and repeat.

Case study

Recreating transitions from examples

I wanted complex transitions to be quick to create. I showed the AI examples of transitions taken from finished edits and guided it, step by step, toward recreating similar transitions in ModCut. Each version was reviewed against the reference until it captured the style, so users can now create that kind of transition quickly.

Case study

Tuning how the AI model reads requests

ModCut uses a downloadable, pre-existing AI model. I did not train it. I guided Claude Code in configuring it so that a request like “presentation” is interpreted in the visual style I intended. Getting there took repeated rounds of testing and feedback before the results became genuinely useful.

My contribution

Who did what.

ModCut was a collaboration between me and AI tools. Here is an honest breakdown of the roles.

My role

  • Product vision. Came up with the idea for ModCut and what it should be.
  • Workflow design. Decided how features should work for the person editing.
  • Direction. Wrote the prompts and chose the reference examples.
  • Testing & review. Tested results and judged whether they met the goal.
  • Decisions. Combined my ideas with AI suggestions and chose what to keep.

The AI tools' role

  • Claude Code and another AI agent carried out the development work based on my prompts and feedback.
  • They also proposed suggestions, some of which I adopted into the final design.
  • The in-app AI model is a pre-existing, downloadable model, configured for ModCut, not trained by me.

My story

How My Vibe Coding Journey Started

About three years ago, when I was 13, I was learning Unity. I made my first game by following a tutorial, without knowing how to code yet.

Later, I discovered that ChatGPT could help write code, so I started experimenting with it on my own projects. It was far from smooth. The code often had bugs or didn't work the way I expected.

So I learned through trial and error. I practised explaining my ideas more clearly, testing what came back, spotting what was wrong, and guiding the AI step by step toward what I had in mind. Sometimes I even worked out how to explain an idea while walking my dog.

The AI helped write the code. I brought the ideas, the direction, the testing, and the persistence.

I'm not an expert programmer, and I didn't build everything on my own. But over time, I got much better at turning ideas into working projects. That experience is what eventually helped me shape ModCut and guide the development of its AI-powered features.
